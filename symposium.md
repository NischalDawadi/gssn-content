# GSSN Nepal Research Symposium 2026

## Research Without Borders: Developing the Next Generation of Academic Clinicians

### Final Symposium Report and Participant Evaluation

**Date:** 29 August 2026  
**Format:** Virtual Symposium

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The **GSSN Nepal Research Symposium 2026** was held virtually on **29 August 2026** under the theme:

> **“Research Without Borders: Developing the Next Generation of Academic Clinicians”**

The symposium provided a platform for **medical students, junior doctors, researchers, and clinicians** to engage with research, academic career development, global surgery, mentorship, medical education, simulation, and healthcare innovation.

The programme also aimed to make academic medicine and research more accessible to early-career researchers and medical students by providing exposure to experienced researchers, academic clinicians, research presentations, mentorship, and opportunities for networking.

A total of **208 participants registered** for the symposium. Medical students represented the overwhelming majority of the registered audience, with **192 participants (92.3%)** identifying as medical students.

The registration data also demonstrated that many participants were relatively early in their research journey:

- **99 (47.6%)** had previously participated in a research study.
- **63 (30.3%)** had written an abstract.
- **29 (13.9%)** had presented research at a symposium or conference.
- **40 (19.2%)** had published a research paper.
- **41 (19.7%)** had previously attended a research symposium.

The virtual meeting reached an attendance of **140 participants**. The post-symposium feedback dataset contained **94 responses**, representing **45.2% of the 208 registered participants** when compared with the registration cohort.

Participant evaluation was strongly positive. Overall symposium quality was rated **4.10/5**, while clarity and relevance of research presentations were rated **4.19/5**. The likelihood of recommending a future GSSN symposium was rated **4.62/5**, with **88 of 94 respondents (93.6%)** providing a rating of 4 or 5.

The qualitative feedback particularly valued **speaker journeys and personal experiences, mentorship, guidance on starting research, and the oral and poster research showcase**.

The principal area for improvement was **time management**, followed by technical and audiovisual issues, requests for greater interaction, and demand for more practical research training.

## 4. Symposium Aim

The symposium was designed to create an accessible academic platform connecting medical students, junior doctors, researchers, and clinicians around research and academic medicine.

The programme focused on:

- Research development
- Academic career development
- Mentorship
- Global surgery
- Medical education
- Simulation
- Healthcare innovation
- International collaboration
- Student research presentation

The overarching objective was to support the development of the **next generation of academic clinicians** by connecting early-career participants with research experiences, academic pathways, mentorship, and opportunities for scientific communication.

---

## 5. Programme and Thematic Structure

The symposium was organized around **four broad themes**, creating a progression from individual research development to leadership, global collaboration, and innovation in healthcare education.

### Theme 1: Starting Your Research Journey

**From Medical Student to Academic Clinician**

This theme focused on:

- Entering research early
- Developing an academic portfolio
- Identifying mentors
- Exploring international research opportunities
- Understanding research as a potential component of a long-term clinical career

Speakers shared their own academic-career experiences and discussed pathways through which students and early-career clinicians can begin developing their research interests.

---

### Theme 2: Leadership, Mentorship & Diversity

**Building the Next Generation of Academic Leaders**

This theme explored:

- Mentorship
- Leadership
- Diversity
- Career planning
- Institutional support for young researchers
- Research networks

The sessions highlighted the role of mentorship and academic networks alongside formal research training in supporting early-career researchers.

---

### Theme 3: Global Surgery Research & International Collaboration

**Improving Equitable Surgical Care Through Partnerships**

This theme addressed:

- International collaboration
- Multicentre research
- Implementation science
- Research capacity building
- Locally led research
- Equitable research partnerships
- Sustainable international collaboration

Particular emphasis was placed on developing partnerships that support locally led research rather than one-directional exchange.

---

### Theme 4: Medical Education, Simulation & Healthcare Innovation

**Technology & Innovation Transforming Healthcare Training**

This theme connected research with:

- Evidence-based medical education
- Simulation-based learning
- Virtual reality
- Medical engineering
- National standards for simulation education
- Artificial Intelligence

The sessions explored implications for:

- Healthcare training
- Patient safety
- Access to quality education
- Healthcare innovation

## 18. Integrated Findings

### 1. Strongly Student-Centred Symposium

Both the registration and feedback datasets demonstrate that medical students formed the overwhelming majority of the audience.

### 2. High Research Motivation with Lower Practical Confidence

Participants demonstrated strong interest in research and recognized its importance to their careers and clinical practice. However, confidence in practical research tasks was comparatively lower.

### 3. Mentorship as a Major Value Proposition

Personal research journeys and mentorship were among the most frequently valued components of the symposium.

### 4. Research Presentation as a Meaningful Component

The oral and poster showcase was valued as both exposure to current research and an example of scientific communication.

### 5. Programme Pacing as the Main Operational Challenge

Time management, delays and programme length clearly dominated the improvement comments.

### 6. Demand for Active Learning

Requests for practical research training and greater Q&A and audience interaction suggest a preference for **workshop-based and participatory learning alongside presentations**.

### 7. Strong Future Demand

Recommendation likelihood was **4.62/5**, with **93.6% of respondents rating their likelihood of recommending a future GSSN symposium at 4 or 5**.

---

## 19. Recommendations for GSSN Research Symposium 2027

Based on the participant evaluation and integrated findings, the following recommendations are proposed:

### 1. Continue Mentorship and Research Journeys

Continue inviting experienced researchers and academic clinicians to share their research journeys, career development experiences and practical advice.

### 2. Introduce Beginner-Level Research Workshops

Develop structured beginner-level training for students interested in research, with a pathway progressing from:

**Research idea → research question → protocol → data collection → analysis → abstract → manuscript → publication**

### 3. Regulate the Number of Speakers

Set realistic speaking times and strictly regulate the number of speakers to reduce delays.

A **two-day format** could be considered if the programme is expanded substantially.

### 4. Increase Audience Interaction

Audience engagement could be strengthened through:

- Live polls
- Structured Q&A
- Chatbox interaction
- Interactive case discussions
- Dedicated discussion periods

### 5. Strengthen Technical Preparation

Technical preparation should include advance testing of:

- Microphones
- Prerecorded videos
- Screen sharing
- Presentation transitions
- Internet connectivity
- Backup presentation files

Contingency plans should be established for technical disruptions.

### 6. Maintain the Research Showcase

The oral and poster research presentations should be retained while ensuring adequate time for:

- Presentation
- Discussion
- Questions
- Feedback
- Recognition

### 7. Broaden but Balance the Content

Future programmes can combine:

- Research methodology
- Global surgery
- Simulation
- Artificial Intelligence
- Public health
- Health equity
- Clinical specialties
- Surgical outcomes
- Nepal and LMIC-specific research

The programme should maintain a balance between **foundational research skills and emerging areas of research and healthcare innovation**.

---

## 20. Overall Programme Significance

The **GSSN Nepal Research Symposium 2026** provided a platform for students and early-career professionals to engage with research and academic medicine across multiple disciplines and settings.

Its strongly student-centred audience, high research interest and positive recommendation ratings demonstrate substantial engagement with the symposium format.

The programme also highlighted an important educational need: participants are motivated to participate in research but require further opportunities to develop **practical research skills, mentorship relationships and confidence in conducting and communicating research**.

The symposium therefore provides a foundation for future GSSN research programming that can move beyond awareness and inspiration toward **structured research capacity building and hands-on academic development**.
