## Overview

This course was conducted on September 1, 2025, using a prospective, mixed-methods design with pre-test and post-test evaluation. It integrated quantitative assessment of surgical skills with qualitative evaluation of participant experience, perception, and satisfaction with the hybrid virtual course design.

The evaluation framework included: Baseline (pre-course) assessment, Immediate post-course assessment, and Post-course debrief session involving faculty and participants.

## Process

### Participants

- **Total participants:** 10 medical students
- **Academic level:** Years 3–5 (all had completed surgical clinical rotations)
- **Gender ratio:** Female-to-male ratio of 3:2
- **Recruitment:** Global Surgery Simulation Network (GSSN)
- **Inclusion criteria:** Medical students (Year 3 and above), Ability to attend the full course duration
- **Exclusion criteria:** Prior participation in similar surgical skills courses, Inability to travel or complete the session

### Course Setting

- Venue: Local high school with an adequately sized, well-lit training hall
- Workstations: Individual stations with standardized equipment
- Faculty support: Remote faculty from the UK (live virtual mentoring), Two on-site orthopedic consultants for local supervision

### Course Structure

Format: One-day hybrid course. Time zones were carefully coordinated, with UK faculty joining during their morning and Nepal afternoon sessions.

**Morning session (Nepal):**
- Prerecorded video lectures
- Gloving and gowning
- Instrument handling
- Knot tying
- Skin suturing (simple interrupted and vertical mattress)

**Afternoon session:**
- Local anesthetic infiltration
- Cyst and abscess removal
- Skin lesion excision
- Bowel anastomosis
- Tendon repair

### Simulation Materials

Each participant received a standardized kit containing:
- Surgical instruments, sutures, and needles
- Porcine abdominal specimen (10 × 15 inches with nipple)
- Porcine trotter with exposed Achilles tendon
- Caprine intestinal tissue with mesentery (15 cm)

### Instruction & Tele-Mentoring

- Skills were first demonstrated via prerecorded videos projected on-site
- Videos could be replayed multiple times
- Live mentoring was conducted through Microsoft Teams breakout rooms
- Split-screen camera setup enabled real-time feedback
- Mandatory earphones ensured clear two-way communication
- Technical support team and backup materials were available throughout

### Assessment & Data Collection

- Quantitative assessment: OSATS checklist, Video-recorded suturing and knot-tying tasks (Scored independently by two blinded expert surgeons)
- Qualitative assessment: Post-course structured questionnaire (Likert scales + open-ended), Evaluation of confidence/satisfaction, Observational field notes

## Impact

- Demonstrated feasibility of delivering high-quality surgical skills training using a hybrid model in a low-resource setting all the way from UK to Nepal
- Enabled real-time international faculty mentorship without geographical barriers
- Improved participant confidence and engagement in core surgical skills
- Provided a scalable and cost-effective framework for surgical education in LMICs
- Generated evidence to support wider adoption of hybrid simulation-based surgical training programs
