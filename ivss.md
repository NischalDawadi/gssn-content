## Program Overview

The **International Virtual Surgical Skills Series (IVSS-Series)** was the first flagship educational program of the Global Surgery Simulation Network (GSSN). This faculty-led, fully virtual initiative was designed to introduce medical students to fundamental surgical knot-tying skills using a simple, accessible, and resource-efficient approach.

Conducted from **January 21 to February 8, 2025**, the series aligned with GSSN’s mission of promoting equitable surgical education, particularly in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs).


## Technologies & Methods Utilized
- **Microsoft Teams**: Used for live instruction and real-time interaction.
- **Suture Centre, Hull**: Prerecorded instructional videos.
- **Live Demonstrations**: Conducted by the Founder/President of GSSN.
- **Low-Cost Materials**: Basic household items (cup and thread) were used for hands-on practice, proving that essential competencies can be taught without expensive equipment.



## Program Curriculum

#### Part 1: Foundations (January 21, 2025)
Focused on the **Reef Knot**, covering foundational principles such as hand positioning, knot security, and tension control.
**Participants:** 100+ medical students

#### Part 2: Advanced Knots (January 29, 2025)
Introduced the **Single-Handed and Double-Handed Surgeon’s Knots**, emphasizing efficiency, dexterity, and adaptability in surgical settings.
**Participants:** 60+ medical students

#### Part 3: Depth & Precision (February 8, 2025)
Dedicated to **Deep Tissue Knot Tying**, highlighting spatial awareness and knot stability in depth.
**Participants:** 60+ medical students


## Participant Data & Reach
- **Total Reach:** 100+ unique participants across all sessions.
- **Completion Rate:** 40+ students completed all three parts.
- **Target Audience:** Medical students (MBBS Years 1–5).
- **Geographic Reach:** International (Multi-country participation).

## Impact
The IVSS-Series demonstrated that high-quality surgical training can be **democratized** through low-cost, scalable virtual models. Key impacts include:

1.  **Early Skill Acquisition:** Introducing knot-tying early in medical training.
2.  **Resource Independence:** Enabling skill acquisition without specialized simulation equipment.
3.  **Confidence Building:** Nurturing interest in surgery among medical students globally.
4.  **Scalability:** Establishing a replicable model that directly led to the development of the **Keep No Operation Tangled (K.N.O.T.) Campaign**.
