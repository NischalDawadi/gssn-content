## Overview

This study evaluated an International Surgical Skills Course (ISSC) delivered as a one-day, in-person simulation-based program using a prospective mixed-methods pre–post design. A convergent approach integrated quantitative assessments of technical performance and knowledge with qualitative evaluation of participant experience, acceptability, feasibility, and implementation barriers.

The course was jointly organized by the **Global Surgical Simulation Network (GSSN)** in collaboration with **APF Hospital**, with principal surgical tutors from the United Kingdom. Instruction followed a **Royal College of Surgeons of Edinburgh (RCSEd)** adapted stepwise framework comprising theory, live demonstration, and guided hands-on practice, ensuring structured skill acquisition aligned with RCSEd standards in a resource-limited setting.

## Participants

- **Total participants:** 31
- **Professional background:** Undergraduate medical students, medical officers, surgical residents, and allied healthcare professionals.
- **Support team:**
  - 4 course coordinators
  - 6 faculty assessors
  - 6 organizing committee members
  - 7 volunteers

#### Eligibility Criteria

**Inclusion criteria:**

- Voluntary participation via prior registration
- Baseline familiarity with surgical principles
- Commitment to attend the full-day program

**Exclusion criteria:**

- Inability to participate in hands-on practice
- Contraindications to handling animal tissue

_Ethical Note: Written informed consent was obtained prior to OSATS assessments and questionnaire administration. Registration was paper-based and collected demographic information, professional role, prior surgical exposure, and previous simulation experience. All data were anonymized before analysis._

## Course Design and Structure

- **Format:** One-day, in-person simulation-based course
- **Venue:** Skills laboratory, APF Hospital
- **Teaching model:** RCSEd-adapted stepwise approach (theory → demonstration → supervised practice)
- **Learning approach:** All participants progressed through modules together in a fixed sequence, ensuring uniform exposure, consistent teaching, and peer-supported learning.

## Course Modules

### Module 1: Gowning and Gloving

Introduction to sterility and infection prevention in the operating theatre. Faculty demonstrated correct gowning and gloving techniques with emphasis on maintaining the sterile field. Participants practiced under supervision to establish safe operative behavior.

### Module 2: Knot Tying

Training included reef knots, surgeon’s knots (one-handed and two-handed), and tying at depth. Low-cost, student-made **Zig Box** simulators were used to facilitate repetitive practice. Emphasis was placed on hand positioning, knot security, and efficiency within defined time frames.

### Module 3: Suturing

Tutors introduced surgical instruments, correct handling techniques, and common errors. Suturing practice was performed on pork belly tissue using vicryl cutting-tip sutures. Techniques included simple interrupted, simple continuous, vertical mattress, and subcuticular sutures.

### Module 4: Bowel Anastomosis

Participants performed end-to-end bowel anastomosis on porcine bowel loops using round-bodied PDS sutures. Instruction emphasized luminal alignment, appropriate bite placement, and creation of a watertight closure.

### Module 5: Tendon Repair

Using a porcine trotter, tutors demonstrated tendon handling and repair principles, focusing on tension-free approximation and prevention of gapping. Participants practiced stepwise repairs with immediate feedback.

### Module 6: Laparoscopic Simulation

Laparoscopic skills were introduced using the validated **LaApSi model**, a low-cost training tool suitable for LMICs. Psychomotor exercises included sugar cube stacking, polo-ring transfer, suture threading through rings, and precision cutting tasks. Teaching focused on ergonomics, instrument triangulation, depth perception, and safe tissue handling.

## Instruction and Faculty Roles

- **Principal tutors:** Delivered theoretical instruction, live demonstrations, and supervised practice.
- **Volunteers:** Trained through RCSEd-accredited CPD programs; provided close hands-on support and real-time feedback.
- **Faculty assessors:** Registered surgeons who determined CPD certification eligibility.
- **Program coordinator:** Oversaw logistics, orientation, and workflow.

## Assessment and Data Collection

### Quantitative Assessment

- **Objective Structured Assessment of Technical Skills (OSATS):** Conducted pre- and post-course.
- **Domains assessed:** Accuracy, ergonomics, instrument handling, and reproducibility.
- **Surveys:** Knowledge and confidence assessed using structured pre- and post-course surveys.

### Qualitative Assessment

- **Questionnaires:** Structured items with Likert-scale and open-ended questions.
- **Field notes:** Observational notes documenting engagement, error patterns, and faculty reflections.
- **Debriefing:** In-person post-course debriefing held three days after the course to explore feasibility, acceptability, and barriers.

## Data Analysis

- **Software:** Quantitative data analyzed using SPSS.
- **Descriptive statistics:** Summarized demographics, survey responses, and costs.
- **Comparative analysis:** Pre–post comparisons performed using paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, with effect sizes calculated.
- **Thematic analysis:** Qualitative data analyzed by two independent researchers, with discrepancies resolved through consensus.

## Ethical Considerations

Participation was voluntary and had no impact on academic or professional evaluation. Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Review Board of APF Hospital for retrospective analysis and publication. The study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki, with strict biosafety protocols for PPE use, sharps handling, and disposal of biological tissues.

## Rigor and Trustworthiness

Credibility was ensured through triangulation of OSATS scores, survey data, and observations. Standardized teaching protocols and low-cost simulation models supported dependability and transferability. Reflexivity and reliability were maintained through faculty review, independent coding, and consensus discussions.

## Impact

The ISSC demonstrated the feasibility, effectiveness, acceptability, and scalability of RCSEd-aligned simulation-based surgical training in an LMIC setting. Inclusion of validated low-cost laparoscopic simulation highlighted a sustainable and reproducible model for multidisciplinary surgical education in resource-limited contexts.
