## Course Overview

The Keep No Operation Tangled (K.N.O.T.) campaign was a pilot initiative by the Global Surgery Simulation Network (GSSN). Running from January 21 to July 2, 2025, it launched with the International Virtual Surgical Skill Series (IVSS-Series). The campaign's primary goal was to leverage a peer-to-peer teaching model to instruct medical students in essential surgical skills: basic surgical knot tying (including the Single-Handed Reef Knot, Single-Handed Surgeon’s Knot, Double-Handed Surgeon’s Knot, Instrument Tying, and Tying-at-Depth) and sterile gloving technique.

## Campaign Description

The K.N.O.T. Campaign was executed in two phases, with Phase 1 subdivided into three steps.

### Phase 1: Cascading Peer-Teaching Model

- **Step 1**: Faculty from the UK trained and assessed 18 GSSN "Medical Representatives" from 18 different colleges via Microsoft Teams.
- **Step 2**: These trained Representatives then taught basic surgical knots and sterile gloving to GSSN general members at their respective colleges through in-person and virtual sessions.
- **Cross-Assessment**: To ensure competency, the newly trained general members were cross-assessed by Medical Representatives from other colleges. Those who passed progressed to Phase 2.

### Phase 2: Peer-to-Peer Expansion

Participants who passed the cross-assessment became instructors, teaching the skills to other general members and non-members, thereby expanding the campaign's reach.

- **Medical Colleges Involved**: 18
- **Total Training Hours Delivered**: ~236 hours
- **Total Assessment Hours Conducted**: 66 hours

## Impact

The K.N.O.T. Campaign demonstrates a scalable and sustainable model for surgical skill dissemination, particularly relevant for low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). Its impacts include:

- **Democratizing Access**: Making high-quality surgical training accessible to a wider student population.
- **Building Local Capacity**: Empowering local student leaders to become effective instructors.
- **Enhancing Early Training**: Introducing critical surgical skills early in the medical curriculum.
- **Hybrid Learning Model**: Successfully combining virtual mentoring with local, hands-on practice.
- **Foundation for Research**: Providing a robust dataset for ongoing medical education research.
