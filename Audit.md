# Clinical Audit & Quality Improvement Project (QIP) Workshop

## Research & Quality Improvement Capacity-Building Session for Undergraduate Medical Students

*A structured academic workshop conducted by the GSSN EDI Wing with support from the Education Wing to strengthen foundational and applied competencies in clinical audit and quality improvement, from identifying quality gaps and designing audit cycles to applying PDSA methodology and developing practical improvement projects.*

---

## 1. Programme Overview

As part of its commitment to strengthening practical clinical and quality-improvement competencies among undergraduate medical students, the Global Surgical Students Network (GSSN) conducted the academic session:

**“Clinical Audit & Quality Improvement Project (QIP) Workshop”**

The session was designed to bridge the gap between theoretical understanding and practical application of clinical audit and quality improvement methodology. Participants were introduced to the distinctions between clinical audit, QIP, and research, the clinical audit cycle, and the Plan-Do-Study-Act (PDSA) framework.

Particular emphasis was placed on practical application through a simulated audit exercise, enabling participants to collaboratively develop a quality-improvement project from identifying a problem through standard-setting, data collection, analysis, intervention, and re-audit.

---

## 2. Core Focus Areas

The workshop addressed key components of clinical audit and quality improvement, including:

- Understanding the differences between clinical audit, QIP, and research
- Understanding the clinical audit cycle
- Identifying quality and patient-safety problems
- Understanding the Plan-Do-Study-Act (PDSA) framework
- Designing clinical audit and QIP projects
- Applying quality-improvement methodology to practical scenarios
- Understanding data collection, analysis, and interpretation
- Translating identified gaps into practical improvement strategies
- Developing confidence to participate in future audit and QIP projects

---

## 3. Methodological Objectives

The session aimed to enable participants to:

1. Understand the fundamental principles of clinical audit and quality improvement.
2. Distinguish between clinical audit, QIP, and research.
3. Identify and structure appropriate quality and patient-safety problems.
4. Understand and apply the clinical audit cycle.
5. Explain and apply the Plan-Do-Study-Act (PDSA) framework.
6. Develop a basic clinical audit or QIP project from scratch.
7. Understand approaches to data collection, analysis, and interpretation.
8. Develop greater confidence in undertaking future audit and quality-improvement projects.

The session emphasised both conceptual understanding and practical execution, with particular focus on applying quality-improvement methodology to realistic clinical scenarios.

## 6. Session Delivery

The workshop was delivered online through video conferencing and followed an interactive, academically focused format combining structured teaching with practical group work.

The session incorporated:

- Concept-based teaching on clinical audit, QIP, and research
- Introduction to the clinical audit cycle
- Teaching on the PDSA framework
- Practical discussion of quality and patient-safety problems
- Small-group breakout activities
- Simulated clinical audit design
- Facilitator feedback and discussion
- Application of theoretical concepts to practical scenarios

During the simulated audit exercise, participants worked in small breakout groups to design a dummy audit, progressing from standard-setting through to re-audit. Facilitators moved between groups to provide guidance and feedback.

This format allowed participants to connect methodological concepts with practical quality-improvement applications.

## 8. Programme Evaluation & Outcomes

A single-arm pre- and post-workshop evaluation was conducted using participant questionnaires. A total of **26 medical students from 12 colleges** participated in the workshop. Of the responses received, **23 participants provided matched pre- and post-workshop responses**, forming the basis of the paired analysis.

### Knowledge Outcomes

| Knowledge Domain | Pre-Workshop | Post-Workshop | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Clearly understand the difference between audit, QIP and research | 4.3% | 87.0% | +82.7 percentage points |
| Correctly identify the audit cycle | 47.8% | 100% | +52.2 percentage points |
| Can explain the PDSA framework | 8.7% | 100% | +91.3 percentage points |

The results demonstrated improvement across all three assessed knowledge domains.

### Confidence Outcomes

| Competency | Pre-Workshop Mean /5 | Post-Workshop Mean /5 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Identifying quality and safety problems | 2.87 | 4.00 | +1.13 |
| Designing a QIP/audit from scratch | 2.04 | 4.30 | +2.26 |
| Data collection, analysis and interpretation | 2.61 | 4.22 | +1.61 |

The largest increase in self-rated confidence was observed in designing an audit or QIP from scratch, increasing from **2.04 to 4.30 out of 5**.

### Willingness to Participate in Future Projects

Willingness to lead or participate in future audit or QIP work increased from:

**43.5% → 87.0%**

This represented an increase from **10 to 20 participants** among the 23 matched respondents.

### Participant Satisfaction

| Evaluation Measure | Result |
|---|---:|
| Rated the workshop 5/5 | 73.9% |
| Rated the workshop 4/5 | 26.1% |
| Session exceeded expectations | 52.2% |
| Session met expectations | 43.5% |
| Session somewhat met expectations | 4.3% |
| Agreed the session improved understanding and confidence | 95.7% |
| Would recommend the workshop to a peer | 100% |

## 9. Participant Feedback

Participants particularly valued:

---

## 12. Limitations

The programme evaluation had several limitations:

- The evaluation used a single-arm pre- and post-workshop design without a control group.
- The observed changes therefore cannot be attributed to the workshop with certainty.
- Outcomes were self-reported and measured immediately after the session.
- Long-term knowledge retention and real-world behaviour change were not assessed.
- The paired sample was relatively small, with **23 matched participants**.
- Only descriptive statistics were used, and no formal significance testing was performed.

---

## 13. Recommendations and Next Steps

Based on participant feedback and evaluation findings, future iterations may consider:

- Improving time management and session pacing.
- Incorporating additional case-based scenarios and practical examples.
- Increasing the number of simulated audit exercises.
- Conducting follow-up assessments to evaluate longer-term retention and application.
- Strengthening evaluation through a larger matched sample.
- Applying paired statistical tests in future evaluations.
- Connecting interested participants with mentors and ongoing audit/QIP projects through GSSN wings.
